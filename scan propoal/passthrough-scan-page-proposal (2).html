Passthrough · Scan page proposal

One shell. The whole journey.

Today scanning is a card halfway down the marketing homepage, so a signed-in person who clicks "Scan free" leaves the app they are in. The proposal gives scanning its own address, /scan, that wears the dashboard's shell when you are signed in and a quiet focus shell when you are not, and keeps that shell all the way through: scan, score, payment, rewrite and apply. The result is a short run of tabs that unlock as the person earns them.

What the code does today

Six things that make the scan page feel borrowed

Read from the current repo (commit fef9cad), not from memory of it.

01There is no /scan page

The router has /scan/:id but no /scan, so that address is a 404. "Scan free" points at /#scan-form, the card inside the homepage hero (Navbar.jsx).

02Signed-in people are sent to the marketing site

The public navbar (How it works, Stories, Pricing, For employers) wraps the homepage and every /scan/:id result (ScanResult.jsx), exactly as in your screenshot. The dashboard tabs disappear the moment you scan.

03One form, three different homes

The same ScanForm lives in the homepage hero, in the dashboard's "Check a new job" accordion, and behind /?from=. Signed-in people inside the dashboard still read "No account needed."

04Deep links land on the pitch

Six inbound links (/?from=, /?mode=, /?profile= from the scan menu, results and Settings) open the homepage at the top. On a phone the form sits under the headline and proof.

05Problems are found after the click

The LinkedIn block-list lives only on the server (jd.parser.js), the anonymous 1-per-hour limit is learned from a 429, and a used-up day reads "resets at midnight UTC" while the dashboard shows your local time. None of these tell you what to do next.

06Two brand treatments

The public bar uses the logo mark and Bricolage wordmark; the dashboard header is a plain blue text "Passthrough". Same product, two identities, and the scan flow crosses both.

The logic

Three shells, chosen by one rule

Whoever you are, you get the chrome that matches your job at that moment. The marketing bar is for deciding; the app bar is for doing.

RouteSigned outSigned in
/ homepageMarketing bar, hero scan card stays (conversion engine)Marketing bar, hero card becomes "Start a scan" → /scan
/scan newFocus shell: logo + Sign inApp shell: dashboard tabs + New scan
/scan/:id + tabsFocus shell (was marketing bar)App shell (was marketing bar)
/dashboard/*→ sign inApp shell, New scan button on every tab

Why this converts better

  • One decision per screen. The page asks for two things, a resume and a job, and shows a rail so the finish line is visible.
  • No dead ends. The button is never silently grey: the bar beside it says "Add the job to continue". A used-up day offers the next useful action instead of an error.
  • Repeat scans cost seconds. Returning people see their recent resumes and saved profile first. The product's real loop is one resume, many jobs.
  • Honest about limits up front. Anonymous visitors see "1 an hour, kept 24 hours" before they invest, with the account upgrade next to it.
  • The homepage keeps its job. It stays the only indexed entry; /scan stays noindex so there is no duplicate-content split.
The logic

Status decides the tabs. The URL decides where you land.

The page already branches on the scan's status; this just makes the branches visible.

Error, refund, disputereplaced by a plain noticethe notice

Rules that keep it honest

  1. Tabs only appear. Nothing the person earned is hidden later.
  2. No greyed-out locked tabs; the offer card does the selling.
  3. Each tab has an address: /scan/:id, /why, /resume, /apply. Emails link to the right one.
  4. Auto-switch only right after payment or when a fix finishes. Never while someone is typing in the editor.
  5. The Score tab always holds the one thing to do next.
  6. Failures replace the tab area, so a refund or error is never buried.
The design language

Instrument clarity
